Teacher Guide

Life Cycle Modeler teacher guide

Use this guide to turn the app into a structured lesson about stage-specific vulnerability, demographic leverage, and evidence-based management reasoning.

Open application Learner guide Standards

Quick Start

Recommended band

Upper high school through introductory college ecology, evolution, or conservation biology.

Typical duration

One 25-40 minute investigation or a longer paired lesson with demographic comparison and CER writing.

Device assumptions

Laptop or tablet preferred. The workbench is responsive, but comparing several runs is easier on a wider screen.

Teacher Moves

Set up the question

  • Ask which life stage should matter most to persistence and why.
  • Use a familiar organism so the stage labels are meaningful.
  • Keep stage structure visible, not just the total population.

During the run

  • Require one parameter change at a time.
  • Have learners justify whether survival or fecundity changed the projection more.
  • Ask what management or conservation implication would follow from the result.

Suggested Evidence Tasks

Prompt

Which stage has the greatest leverage on persistence in your comparison: juvenile survival, adult survival, or fecundity? Use the projection outputs as evidence.

Listen for

  • Recognition that stage-specific effects can outweigh whole-population intuitions.
  • Claims justified with projection behavior rather than only prior expectation.
  • Willingness to revise the “most important stage” claim after comparison.
