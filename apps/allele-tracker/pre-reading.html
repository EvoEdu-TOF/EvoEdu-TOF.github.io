Pre-Reading: Core Mechanisms of Evolution

Excerpt from the Notebook On Evolution, Section III

Adaptive vs. Non-Adaptive Change

Not all evolutionary change is driven by natural selection:

  • Adaptive change is shaped by natural or sexual selection to enhance fitness.
  • Non-adaptive change arises from genetic drift, mutation bias, developmental constraints, or historical contingency.

Core Mechanisms

Genetic Drift

Random change in allele frequencies due to sampling error, especially in small populations. Drift can cause alleles to fix or be lost even if they are neutral (neither beneficial nor harmful). This is the dominant force in the Allele Tracker when selection is set to zero.

Natural Selection

Differential survival and reproduction of individuals with certain heritable traits. In population genetics models, selection is quantified by a selection coefficient (s). Even weak selection can overcome drift in large populations.

Gene Flow (Migration)

The movement of alleles between populations via migration. Gene flow homogenizes populations and can introduce new variation or counteract local selection.

Mutation

The ultimate source of genetic variation. Mutation rates are typically low, but they determine the input of new alleles into a population.

Neutral Theory

Motoo Kimura proposed that most evolutionary change at the molecular level is due to drift acting on neutral mutations—not selection. The Allele Tracker can model this by setting selection to zero and observing fixation due to drift alone.
